# LinkedIn Sketch Video — Production Brief

*"Getting your morning back" — a 20-second whiteboard sketch animation for Algarve Property Compliance.*

---

## Why Sketch Art for LinkedIn

Sketch art is the right style for LinkedIn because **it doesn't look like an advert**.

It looks like someone doodling an idea on a whiteboard — which is exactly why people stop scrolling. We lean into that: no polish, no product shots, no logos. A hand-drawn story that lands the feeling first and lets the viewer join the dots.

**Guiding rules for the whole video:**

- Simple whiteboard style — black line drawings with occasional soft colour highlights.
- Light, relatable, slightly humorous.
- **No company logos. No branding. No mention of AI or software.**
- The story is about one thing: **getting time back.**

---

## The Prompt

> Create a 20-second hand-drawn sketch animation in a simple whiteboard style with black line drawings and occasional soft colour highlights. Keep it light, relatable and slightly humorous. No company logos, no branding, no mention of AI or software. The story should be about getting time back.

---

## Scene-by-Scene Script

### Scene 1 — 0:00–0:05 · "Not that long ago…"

- A busy office worker sitting at a desk.
- An email arrives: **"Can you send me a quotation?"**
- The character sighs.
- Papers pile up.
- The clock spins from **9:00am to 12:00pm**.
- Coffee goes cold.
- The character rubs their head, looking exhausted.

**Caption:** *"Not that long ago…"*

> **The gag (≈2 seconds):** As the clock jumps from 9:00am to 12:00pm, the coffee cup changes from steaming hot to completely empty — with a cobweb (or a little fly) floating above it. People instantly understand the message without a word of explanation. This is the memorable, shareable touch — keep it in.

### Scene 2 — 0:05–0:10 · "Most of the morning"

- The screen fills with paperwork, folders, spreadsheets and checklists.
- The character is buried underneath them.

**Caption:** *"One quotation could take most of the morning."*

### Scene 3 — 0:10–0:17 · The turn

- Everything suddenly clears.
- The same character simply takes a **screenshot of the enquiry**.
- They drag it into a computer.
- A few seconds later a neat document appears with green ticks beside:
  - ✓ Professional quotation
  - ✓ Clear explanation of what's included
  - ✓ Example inspection report attached
- The character smiles, grabs their coat, and walks out of the office carrying an **inspection clipboard** instead of paperwork.
- The clock now reads **9:08am**.

### Scene 4 — 0:17–0:20 · The clean ending

- The desk is tidy.
- The paperwork has disappeared.
- The character is outside, happily carrying out an inspection, with the office window visible in the background.

**Final caption:**

> *"The best technology isn't the technology you notice…"*
>
> *(pause)*
>
> *"…it's the technology that quietly gives you your morning back."*

Fade out.

**No logo. No company name. No call to action.** Leave the ending clean and thought-provoking.

## Voiceover Script

**Mute-first, listen-along.** The on-screen captions carry the story for the ~85% of LinkedIn who watch on mute. This voiceover is the layer for anyone who taps the sound on — so it *complements* the captions rather than reading them back word-for-word. It should feel like a colleague telling you something over coffee, not a narrator selling a product.

### Voice & delivery direction

- **Casting:** Female, mid-30s. Match the read used previously — warm, dry, understated, conversational. Natural UK English.
- **Energy:** Low-key and human. This is a quiet observation, not an announcement. No "advert voice," no rising upsell inflection at the ends of lines.
- **Attitude:** A hint of wry humour on the paperwork/coffee beats (she's been there). Genuine warmth on the payoff.
- **Pace:** Unhurried. Let the pauses do work — especially the final line.
- **Mix:** Sit the VO slightly under any light background sound. It should never fight the captions.

### Timed script

| Time | Scene | Voiceover line | Delivery note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | 1 | "Not that long ago… one little question could eat your whole morning." | Easy, almost nostalgic. Small lift on "eat." |
| 0:05–0:10 | 2 | "'Can you send me a quotation?' — and just like that, the paperwork's winning." | Dry, knowing. Slight sigh baked into "winning." |
| 0:10–0:16 | 3 | "Now? You screenshot it, drop it in… and it's done. The quote, the details, even the inspection report." | Brighten and quicken — this is the relief. Land "done" cleanly. |
| 0:16–0:17 | 3 | *(no VO — coat on, out the door; let the picture breathe)* | Silence is deliberate here. |
| 0:17–0:20+ | 4 | "The best technology isn't the kind you notice… it's the kind that quietly gives you your morning back." | Slower, softer — almost to herself. Full beat on the ellipsis. Last line may carry ~1s into the fade; that's intended. |

> **Total spoken words: ~55.** Comfortable for a relaxed 20s read with real pauses. If the final line feels rushed, start it at ~0:16.5 and let the fade run to ~0:21 — protect the pause, not the exact frame count.

## Production Notes

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Total length** | 20 seconds (VO may carry ~1s into the fade) |
| **Style** | Whiteboard hand-drawn, black lines, occasional soft colour highlights |
| **Tone** | Light, relatable, slightly humorous |
| **Colour use** | Sparing — reserve highlights for the green ticks and small accents |
| **Captions** | Primary channel. Mute-first — must carry the story with no sound (LinkedIn autoplays muted) |
| **Voiceover** | Listen-along layer. Female, mid-30s; match previous read (warm, dry, conversational). Complements captions, doesn't duplicate them |
| **Branding** | None on screen at any point |
| **CTA** | None — the ending stays open |
| **Signature moment** | The cold-coffee / cobweb gag at the 9→12 clock jump |

### Why the details matter

- The **screenshot → drag → neat document** beat is the whole "magic" of the story shown, not told. It never names a tool, so the viewer supplies the meaning themselves.
- The **9:08am** payoff mirrors the earlier 9:00→12:00 spin — the same clock, a totally different morning.
- Ending on a **thought**, not a pitch, is what makes it feel like a whiteboard idea rather than an ad — and what makes people share it.
